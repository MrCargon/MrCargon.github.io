Conway's Game of Life

Four lines of rules, no randomness after the first frame, and the result is Turing complete. Every pattern below is stored as a few dozen characters of text — no images, no data files.

generation 0 population 0 B3/S23

The rule

Each cell looks at its eight neighbours. A live cell with 2 or 3 live neighbours survives; a dead cell with exactly 3 is born; everything else dies. Written B3/S23 — born on 3, survives on 2 or 3.

Every cell must read the previous generation. Updating in place, so a cell sees neighbours that have already moved, produces a different automaton entirely — it is the most common bug in a first implementation, and double buffering is part of the rule rather than an optimisation.

Why it computes

A glider is a packet that travels in a straight line at a known speed: present is 1, absent is 0. A glider gun supplies a clock. Two gliders meeting at the right angle and phase annihilate, and that single fact yields NOT, AND and OR — NAND alone is enough for everything else. Distance is the wiring: a channel one cell too long shifts the collision phase and the gate stops working.

Nothing was added to the rules to allow this. Universality was already present in 1970; it simply took decades to find the arrangement.

The multiverse

B3/S23 is one point in a space of 262,144 birth/survival rules. Switch universes above: HighLife (B36/S23) contains a genuine self-replicator; Seeds (B2/S) lets nothing survive at all and explodes; Day & Night behaves identically on a live background as on a dead one.

Keep going — make the cell values, the neighbourhood and time itself continuous — and you arrive at Lenia, which is running on the Artificial Life page. Same family, opposite ends.

Life inside Life

The OTCA metapixel (Brice Due, 2006) is a Life pattern that behaves like one Life cell. It is 2048×2048 cells wide and one of its generations takes 35,328 of ours. Tile the plane with them and you get Life whose cells are themselves Life, all the way down.

Not simulated here, deliberately: a single 10×10 metapixel board would be about 4×1011 cell updates per meta-generation. That needs Hashlife and a desktop, not a canvas on a portfolio page — and saying so is more useful than shipping something that pretends.

Scale

This page uses a dense grid on the GPU, where every cell updates independently in a fragment shader and costs zero per-frame JavaScript. The alternative is sparse encoding — store only the live cells — which decouples cost from the size of the universe and is what makes a 264 world addressable. Above both sits Hashlife (Gosper, 1984): a quadtree with memoised futures that can leap 2k generations at once, superlinear on repetitive patterns and unremarkable on noise.

Rules and patterns follow the conwaylife.com corpus. Every pattern here is verified by simulation in tests/verify-patterns.cjs rather than trusted — the R-pentomino settles at generation 1103, the acorn at 5206, and diehard vanishes at 130, all measured, not quoted.
